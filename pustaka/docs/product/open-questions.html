Open questions

Eighteen decisions the BRD deliberately refuses to invent, each with the options actually available, the trade-off each one carries, and what it blocks. Answer them one at a time; every answer becomes a row in the decision log.

How to answer

Answer by OQ id, one at a time, in any order — though the commercial block gates the most downstream work, so starting at OQ-01 unblocks the most. An answer does not have to be one of the listed options; the options exist so that "none of these, here is what I actually want" is an easy thing to say. Each answer is written into the decision log as the next D- number, dated the day it was decided, and this page's row flips to Answered → D-nnn.

Commercial

Eight questions, and they are first because nothing else in domain K can be written as a requirement until they are answered. The repository contains no price, plan, customer, contract, service level or support process — only the structural multi-tenancy the schema declares.

OQ-01 · What is GAOS commercially?

Why it is open: the schema declares tenants, plans and budget caps, and the prototype ships two tenant fixtures. Nothing states whether those tenants are strangers who pay, colleagues in one organisation, or an abstraction that will never be exercised. The answer changes how strong authentication has to be, whether tenancy is a security boundary or a convenience, and whether an SLA is being promised to anyone.

OptionConsequence
(A) Hosted multi-tenant SaaSMatches the schema. Requires real authentication, real isolation, billing, and a service level the current single edge machine and single database node cannot support
(B) Self-hosted licenceTenancy becomes a convenience rather than a security boundary. No billing to build. Hardest path to observing whether the core bet in §1.4 is true, because the runs happen on someone else's infrastructure
(C) Open source, paid hosted tierDistribution and credibility for an unproven thesis; the audit trail becomes the paid differentiator. Slowest path to revenue, and the design artifacts become public
(D) Internal platform onlyEverything already true; no commercial work at all. The SaaS framing of this BRD would be formally withdrawn and domain K deleted rather than deferred

OQ-02 · Who is the target customer?

Why it is open: two very different buyers are consistent with the same design. A governance buyer wants the audit trail and the readiness rubric — the refusal to plan against a thin brief is the feature. A throughput buyer wants more shipped per week and experiences the same refusal as friction. The product emphasis, the onboarding, and the answer to OQ-12 all follow from which one this is.

OQ-03 · Is an external auditor a first-class user?

Why it is open: GAOS can already answer "why does this code exist?" by walking from a commit back to a sentence in the back office. Whether anyone outside the tenant is meant to ask that question has never been decided, and it is not a small difference: an auditor persona implies exported evidence bundles, retention beyond the 90-day full-fidelity window, read-only credentials, and a tamper-evidence story for the event stream. None of that is designed.

OQ-04 · What are the plans, and what does each limit?

Why it is open: Tenant.plan is a declared string with no defined values. maxConcurrentTasks and budgetCapUsd exist with no defaults. There is no signup, no plan selection, no usage view, and no billing.

Sub-questionWhat depends on it
Which dimensions does a plan limit — concurrency, spend, tenants, seats, retention, model tier ceiling?Whether the readiness bands and the model tier ladder become commercial levers, which would compromise their safety rationale
Is plan management self-service or operator-mediated?Requirement J-7 and the whole back-office surface for tenants
Is there a free tier, and what does it exclude?Abuse surface: an unbounded free tier with GAOS-paid model calls is an unbounded loss — see OQ-06

OQ-05 · What is the pricing basis?

Why it is open: model spend is a direct cost of goods that varies per task by an order of magnitude — the routing table alone spans three model tiers, and a retry deliberately runs one tier higher than the attempt that failed. A pricing basis that is not coupled to that variance loses money on exactly the customers who use the product most.

OptionTrade-off
Per seatPredictable for the buyer, unbounded downside for GAOS unless a hard budget cap is enforced per tenant — which exists in the design and nowhere in the code
Per task or per workflowAligns with the unit of work the graph already models. A task that retries three times and escalates twice costs multiples of one that does not
Token pass-through plus marginCost-safe and honest; makes the bill unpredictable, and exposes the buyer to the escalation ladder's cost behaviour
Flat platform fee plus bring-your-own-keyRemoves the cost-of-goods problem entirely by moving it to the tenant. Depends on OQ-06

OQ-06 · Who pays the model provider?

Why it is open: it changes what a budget cap means. If GAOS resells, budgetCapUsd protects GAOS's money and must be enforced before dispatch, not reconciled afterwards. If the tenant brings its own key, the cap is a courtesy and the tenant's provider account is the real limit. It also decides whether GAOS ever holds a provider credential belonging to a customer, which is a security obligation with its own requirements.

OQ-07 · What service level and support model?

Why it is open: the honest current position is one public edge machine (1 vCPU, 957 MiB), one private VM, and one ArcadeDB node with no replica. Any uptime commitment made before that changes is a commitment that cannot be met. Tied directly to OQ-16.

OQ-14 · Data residency and tenant data handling

Why it is open: a tenant's repository contents, prompts and diffs pass through GAOS and onward to a model provider. There is no statement of where data is stored, how long, who can read it, whether it may train anything, or what happens on tenant deletion. Retention is specified for operational reasons (90 days of full-fidelity events) but never as a commitment to a customer. Every enterprise buyer asks this in the first meeting.

Product & scope

OQ-08 · What measurable success targets apply?

Why it is open: a standard BRD requires numeric criteria. GAOS has no baseline — no users, no tenants, no run against a real model, and the one defined measurement has never been performed. The realistic sequencing is: run the second-run measurement once, then set targets against what it produces. Confirming that sequencing is the answer to this question.

OQ-12 · Band-driven autonomy, or gates on by default?

Why it is open: the design makes autonomy a function of record completeness — a coherent position, and the whole reason the readiness rubric has teeth. But a new team with a thin brief meets a wall on day one. Turning gates on by default until a tenant opts out is gentler and weakens the incentive that makes the rubric work. This is a product-experience decision, not a safety one; both options keep the money, auth and schema gates.

OQ-15 · What is Mission Control for?

Why it is open: it is the most finished thing in the repository — ported, tested, deployed — and the build order says it should have been ninth. If it is the surface tenants buy, it needs authentication, per-tenant scoping and accessibility work before anything else is added to it. If it is an internal console and the API is the product, it can stay as it is while the loops beneath it get built, which is what the build order implies.

Technical policy

OQ-09 · One model provider, or several?

Why it is open: the model gateway exists as a layer whose stated prohibition is leaking provider-specific shapes upward — the design anticipated multiple providers. Only one is named anywhere, and no second adapter exists. Multi-provider costs a normalised tier abstraction and per-provider cost accounting, and makes the escalation ladder harder to define, because "one tier above" needs a meaning that spans vendors.

OQ-10 · Tenancy strategy at scale

Why it is open: the data model presents all three strategies with their costs and recommends database-per-tenant as the default, but explicitly says to pick one and be strict. Picking late is the expensive outcome — the repository layer is written differently for each.

OptionGood forCost
Database per tenant (the document's default)Hard isolation, per-tenant backup and restore, noisy-neighbour containmentA connection pool per tenant; migrations run N times; cross-tenant analytics needs a separate pipeline
Shared database, tenantId everywhereMany small tenants, cross-tenant reporting, one migrationEvery query must filter, forever. One missing WHERE is a data breach rather than a bug
HybridLarge tenants isolated, small ones pooledTwo code paths in the repository layer; only worth it at scale, and there is no scale yet

OQ-11 · Authentication approach

Why it is open: there is none. The back-office login sets a cookie and checks nothing; any value opens it. This is the single item blocking every commercial requirement, because a tenant boundary without authentication is a diagram. The question is not whether — it is what shape: local accounts, an identity provider, per-tenant SSO, API tokens for machine access, or some combination, and which of those must exist before the first tenant who is not the owner.

OQ-17 · Which ArcadeDB version, validated when?

Why it is open: no DDL has ever been applied. The data model warns in its own words that constraint syntax and index options move between releases, and that EXTERNAL properties require v26.5.1 or later. Pinning a target version and running the schema against a scratch database is cheap now and expensive after the first migration exists — it is build-order step 1's real precondition.

Operations

OQ-13 · Who runs the acceptance tests, and how often?

Why it is open: the design specifies three tests that are the only honest proof the system works — the unsatisfiable task, the fold-from-genesis comparison, and the second run a week apart. It does not say who runs them, on what cadence, or what happens when one fails. The fold test in particular is described as periodic, which implies automation nobody has scheduled.

OQ-16 · Is high availability required before the first external tenant?

Why it is open: HA is discussed in the data model and deferred, with the event stream named as the second recovery path — a restore missing the last few minutes can be rolled forward from events. That is a real mitigation and not the same as availability. Whether a single node is acceptable depends entirely on OQ-01 and OQ-07, which is why this sits behind them.

OQ-18 · Budget caps: initial values and behaviour at the limit

Why it is open: budget_cap is specified per workflow and per tenant and implemented nowhere. It is the highest-consequence unbuilt guard in the system: the first real model key turns an unenforced cap into unbounded financial exposure. Two things need deciding — the initial values, and the behaviour when one is reached. Stopping mid-workflow leaves a half-finished task graph; refusing to dispatch the next task is cleaner but slower to notice; warning and continuing is not a cap.

Nothing here is rhetorical

Every question on this page exists because answering it changes what gets built. None of them is a placeholder, and none can be resolved by reading the repository more carefully — that has already been done, and the absence is the finding.
